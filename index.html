06 — A Little Something

There was still one thing
I wanted to give you.

for you, quietly —

I have never been good at grand gestures, so I made you a quiet one instead. This box holds no object. It holds a decision I keep making, most mornings, without ceremony: to pay attention.

To the way you think before you speak. To the way you go still when something actually matters. To the way an ordinary evening becomes a reference point simply because you were in it — the second cup of coffee, the last word before sleep, the laugh I hear again hours later.

I cannot give you anything you do not already carry. So I give you the one thing that is mine to give: my notice, my admiration, my intention to keep discovering you. Wherever the years put us, I want the seat beside you. Open this whenever the world feels loud. It will always contain the same thing — me, choosing you, on purpose.

— yours

10 — Before You Go

Before you go.

I don't say this often enough in plain words, so let me say it here where I can't interrupt myself.

I noticed you before I had a reason — the way someone notices a change in light. Then admiration followed, specific and growing: how you listen without rehearsing, how you stay yourself in every room, how you make silence comfortable instead of empty.

I kept noticing more. That is the truest sentence in this whole gift. Not one decision, but thousands of small elections of attention — and every one of them lands in the same place. Time with you never feels wasted, even doing nothing. Especially doing nothing.

I realised how naturally my attention returns to you the way you realise a song has been playing for a while — it was already there, underneath everything. My plans quietly reorganised themselves around you. Not from need. From recognition, as if some older part of me had already decided and was simply waiting for the rest to catch up.

I get days wrong. Distracted, short, late in the ways I'm trying to outgrow. You never keep score, and that mercy has changed me more than any lecture could. I want to repay it practically: listening longer, asking better questions, remembering what matters to you without being asked, keeping the small rituals that are only ours.

I understood, somewhere along the way, that admiration can deepen instead of fading. Every year adds resolution — new expressions, new reasons, the same woman, more to learn. I could spend a lifetime studying you and still take notes.

So I chose you. Early, quietly, and again every day since — in silence, in laughter, in ordinary Tuesdays nobody would photograph. And I would choose you again, in every version of my life, the same way: looking until I found you, staying until it meant something.

If years from now someone asks what I was most sure of, I hope the answer is uncomplicated: her. The way she laughed with her whole face. The way home learned her name. The way my attention, out of everyone and everything in this world, kept returning — and still does.

Yours — completely, and without theatre.
